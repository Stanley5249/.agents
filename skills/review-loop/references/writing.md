# Writing checks

Checks for prose in any language. Report only real problems, and skip a check
that finds nothing. Each finding explains a concrete cost to the reader, such as
an unclear subject, a missing logical link, or wording unsuited to the audience.
Treat patterns as clues and judge them in context.

## Logic

- W1 Causality: a claim, choice, or result has a clear reason where one is
  needed. The reason can be in the same sentence or nearby. Flag a link the
  reader must guess.
- W2 Focus: each paragraph has one point that fits in one sentence, and the text
  answers the question or prompt it was written for.
- W3 Weak sentences: a sentence with no evidence, no new information, or only a
  transition can go without losing the point.

## Structure

- W4 One event: each part tells one event or one idea. Mixed content is wrapped
  in one higher-level idea instead of listed.
- W5 Show, not tell: facts carry the claim. Flag self-praise, and endings that
  state a lesson learned where the event should show an ability.

## Language

- W6 Directness: the same meaning said with fewer, plainer words.
- W7 Sentence length: a sentence the reader must read twice, and runs of short
  choppy sentences that should merge.

## Terms

- W8 Inline explanation: a term a general reader cannot follow gets a few words
  of explanation at its first mention, inside the sentence. Each section that
  can be read alone, such as a form field, explains its own terms.

## Content

- W9 Repetition: a repeated word, and the same point stressed more than once.
- W10 Dates and numbers: a date that does not help the reader follow the order
  of events, and numbers written in different formats.
- W11 Claim strength: a claim stronger than the record supports, such as
  "familiar with" for something only learned, or one the author would struggle
  to defend in a follow-up question.

## Consistency

- W12 Cross-check: the same names, facts, and terms across sections, related
  documents, and any source the text must match, such as a job description.

## Rules for suggestions

- Ground rewrites in supported facts from the record and preserve claim
  strength. Return unresolved factual questions to the coordinating reviewer.
- Keep the author's own words listed in the record.
- Follow the "Decided" policy in the record when considering earlier findings.
- A rewrite is the same length or shorter, unless it adds a required reason or
  explanation.
